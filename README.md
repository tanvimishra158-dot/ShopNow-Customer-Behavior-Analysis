The project consists of two homework assignments covering exploratory data analysis, statistical testing, data visualization, and business interpretation.

## Project 1: Exploratory Data Analysis

In the first project, I explored customer transactions, purchasing patterns, product categories, and refund behaviour.

**Key Findings:**

- 2022 recorded the highest total purchase value among the years analysed.
- Electronics generated the highest total purchase value among product categories.
- Approximately 91.9% of transactions were not refunded.
- Most purchases were lower-value transactions, with fewer high-value purchases.

**Skills Demonstrated:** Data exploration, descriptive statistics, data visualization, and interpreting business trends.

## Project 2: Statistical Analysis & Hypothesis Testing

In the second project, I used statistical tests to investigate customer spending, conversion behaviour, and website engagement.

**Key Findings:**

- UK and German customers did not show a statistically significant difference supporting higher UK spending.
- Discount usage and gender were not significantly associated with differences in average transaction value.
- Conversion rates were similar across device types.
- Website browsing activity showed little linear correlation with purchase value.
- Converted visitors spent slightly more time on the website. The difference was statistically significant, but the effect size was small (Cohen's d ≈ 0.10).

**Statistical Methods Used:**

- Welch's Independent Samples t-test
- Chi-square Test of Independence
- Pearson Correlation
- Effect Size Analysis (Cohen's d)

## Business Insights

One of my biggest takeaways from this project was understanding the difference between statistical significance and practical business importance.

A statistically significant result does not always mean that a business should immediately act on it.

I also learned the importance of combining statistical analysis with business context to make more meaningful recommendations.
